Yes — you can still improve, and no, it’s not “all chance.”
But there is always an irreducible randomness layer in greyhound racing (start/bump/trouble).

Most likely missed upside now is in how you use the data, not just adding more rows.

High-value patterns often overlooked:
1. Track-condition regime shifts (same track behaves differently by period/weather/rail state).
2. Pace-shape effects (who leads first split + nearby box speed interactions).
3. Interference risk (dogs with crossing/bumping profiles in crowded lanes).
4. Recency weighting (last 3–5 runs at similar distance/grade matter more than old form).
5. Price-vs-model gap (biggest edge is often when model confidence disagrees with market).
6. Per-track/per-race calibration (which you’ve started) to avoid overconfidence on weak tracks.

So: you are doing a lot right, but there is still room.
Realistically, improvements are usually incremental (small % gains), not massive jumps, and consistency is the main win.
